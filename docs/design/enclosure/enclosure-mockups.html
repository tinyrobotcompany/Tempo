A printed case for the 10.1″ ESP32-P4 display

Three enclosure directions for the Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6-Touch-LCD-10.1 kit. Each one keeps a matte-black front bezel and moves colour into separate printed parts. You can change a colour by reprinting one small part, with no multi-material printer needed. Pick a colourway below to recolour every drawing.

Recommended · Concept C

The Wedge with swappable side cheeks

A desk-clock wedge with a wide, heavy footprint. It stays put when you press the touchscreen, and its rear volume leaves room for the board, cabling and a convection path. The colour sits on two side cheeks and a rear service hatch. Each is a small, flat print you can make in any colour.

  • Most stable under touch of the three; no kickstand to fold or tip.
  • Colour panels are large enough to read across a room, yet each uses little filament.
  • The rear hatch gives access to USB-C, BOOT/RESET and C6 pads without removing the bezel.
  • Every part prints flat on a 256 × 256 mm bed (assumed; see the open questions).

Three concepts

C · Wedge

~264 W × 176 H × ~108 D mm · ≈13° tilt

Colour zones

  • Cheeks ×2 (accent)
  • Service hatch (accent)
  • Rear shell (body)
  • Optional chin inlay line (accent, filament swap)

Strengths

  • Best touch stability and thermal volume
  • Swapping a colour only means reprinting cheeks and the hatch
  • Hides the cable exit at rear-bottom

Trade-offs

  • Deepest footprint (~11 cm)
  • Rear shell is the largest print and is near bed limits
  • Fixed tilt; pick the angle once

A · Slab with trim band

~252 W × 170 H × ~28 D mm · 15° kickstand

Colour zones

  • Trim band around the whole edge (accent)
  • Kickstand (accent)
  • Rear shell (body)

Strengths

  • Slimmest and lightest; reads like a picture frame
  • Trim band gives a crisp colour outline from the front
  • The stand could later be swapped for a wall cleat

Trade-offs

  • Can rock or slide when the top edge is tapped
  • Trim band is a long thin ring that may need splitting into 2–4 segments
  • Little air volume behind the board

B · Cradle base

~268 W × 185 H × ~130 D mm · ≈12° tilt

Colour zones

  • Base block (accent): one big, bold colour mass
  • Display slab back (body)

Strengths

  • Base can carry ballast (steel washers) for a premium, planted feel
  • Slab lifts out for service or wall mounting
  • Strongest single colour statement

Trade-offs

  • Base is the largest single print and needs a lot of filament
  • Cable has to route through the slot joint
  • The slot can loosen over time without a latch

Parts and how they stack

Front to back. The display kit floats on its own M2.5 rear mounts and compliant pads. The bezel never clamps the glass. Cheeks locate on pegs and are held by one captive M3 screw reached from under the foot.

Next steps

From mockup to printable CAD

  1. Measure the assembled kit: glass outline, total thickness, mount-hole pattern, USB-C, power input, BOOT/RESET, C6 pads, antenna position.
  2. Confirm printer model and bed size; decide whether any part needs a split.
  3. Build a parametric model in the repo (OpenSCAD or build123d) driven by those measurements, so colour parts stay interchangeable.
  4. Print a fit-check bezel frame first, then the full set; then run the 72-hour thermal and runtime test from the BOM.
